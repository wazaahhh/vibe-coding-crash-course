# Zoom polls for Crash Course #2 (fallback)

**Primary: the live quiz app** (`apps/live-quiz` in the corp repo, at the URL in `quiz_url.txt`,
QR code on the slides; presenter view at `/present?key=…`). It carries the six polls below plus a
seventh question ("What have you already used AI for in your research?", Part 1). Use these Zoom
polls only if the app is unavailable.

Six **separate** polls, spread through the evening, each where its topic comes up. Create them
in advance on the scheduled meeting (zoom.us → Meetings → the meeting → *Polls/Quizzes*), from
the account that hosts it, and launch one in a test meeting beforehand: polling must be enabled in
the account settings.

**Settings**
- Poll 2 has one correct answer (D); quiz mode is optional. The other five are **opinion polls**:
  plain polls, no quiz mode.
- Poll 1 is *multiple choice* (participants may pick two); the others are *single choice*.
- *Anonymous*: on.
- **During the session:** launch each poll on its slide, end it after ~45 s, **share the results
  straight away** and take one or two reactions. The slide "The room's answers" (Part 7) recaps
  all six: note the majority of each as you go.

The opening question (slide 3) is open-ended: it runs in the **Zoom chat**. Save the chat.

**Poll 1: Where first** *(multiple choice, up to two)*
Where will agentic coding change research practice most in the next five years? Pick up to two.
- A. Analysing data and building models
- B. Designing and running experiments: automated labs
- C. Reading and synthesising the literature
- D. Writing papers and grants
- E. Peer review and replication

**Poll 2: A reality check** *(one correct answer: D)*
In a 2025 randomised trial, experienced open-source developers worked on their own projects with and without AI tools. With AI, they were…
- A. about 40% faster
- B. about 20% faster
- C. no faster and no slower
- D. about 19% slower ✅

**Poll 3: The literature**
Soon an agent can re-run any published analysis from its repository in minutes. What happens to your field's literature?
- A. Replication becomes routine and the literature gets cleaner
- B. A wave of corrections and retractions, then a cleaner literature
- C. Little changes: nobody is rewarded for checking
- D. AI-generated papers multiply faster than anyone can check them

**Poll 4: Training**
A first-year PhD student can now get working analysis code from an agent in minutes. In their first year, they should…
- A. Use agents freely: directing them is the skill that matters now
- B. Write their core analyses by hand first, agents later
- C. Use agents, but explain every line at lab meeting
- D. Keep agents away from thesis-critical analyses

**Poll 5: The scarce skill**
In 2031, the scarcest skill in a neuroscience lab will be…
- A. Programming
- B. Statistics and methods
- C. Choosing the question worth asking
- D. Judging whether a result is true
- E. Collecting good data from real people

**Poll 6: Depth**
Pre-read 1 warns that AI coding could make theoretical neuroscience "prolific but shallow". For your own field, you think…
- A. It is already happening
- B. It will happen unless incentives change
- C. The opposite: faster coding frees time to think
- D. Too early to say
