====== SLIDE 7 — Q4: training the next generation ====== Opinion, and the one where PhD students and PIs tend to split. After the results, ask in the chat: "PhD students: does this match what you'd want? PIs: is this what you do?" DePasquale's argument: the struggle of turning an idea into equations and code is "a form of disciplined thinking"; skip it and you never know your own model. Schuck's lab policy (second pre-read) answers it in practice: trainees do by hand the work that builds core skills (questions, models, arguments) and hand off what they don't need to learn; they draft text themselves before any AI editing. Talmo Pereira (Salk) worries about losing the craft of scientific software. The counter: if directing agents is the job, learn it from day one (A). Comes back: hands-on (17:40), where pairs direct an agent in a challenge repo.

====== SLIDE 8 — Q5: the literature ====== Opinion. The futures question with the widest spread. Push: A and C are the same technology with opposite incentives. Who would pay for the checking? (Funders, journals, an AI reviewer, nobody?) Voice from the pre-read: Dan Goodman (Imperial College London) argues that publication incentives already reward producing models over understanding them, and that vibe modelling could make this worse. That is C and D; A needs someone to reward the checking. Comes back: how the hack runs (18:35): every team's repository is re-runnable by the jury, which is A, at the scale of one weekend.

====== SLIDE 9 — Q6: a reality check ====== The one question with an answer: D. Reveal it now, right after the poll closes. Tasks took 19% longer with AI. Before starting, the developers forecast a 24% speed-up; afterwards they still estimated AI had made them ~20% faster. Caveat to say out loud: early-2025 tools, experts in large mature repositories they knew intimately. METR says the result may not hold for small new projects or unfamiliar code, which is closer to a 32-hour hack. Why it closes the quiz: five minutes of imagining the future, then evidence that our sense of what AI does for us can be wrong in sign. Imagine boldly; measure before you believe. Comes back: review hunt (18:15).
