# Resources — Vibe Coding for Scientists

A curated, annotated reading list to go with the crash course. Start with the
Nature pieces if you're new; dip into the arXiv papers and the cognitive-science
references if you want the evidence behind the slides.

## Vibe coding in science (start here)

- **How to vibe code in science: early adopters share their tips** — *Nature* (2026).
  The best survey of researchers actually doing this: Argonne's 200-person
  hackathon, the "extremely competent graduate student" framing, why precise
  prompts beat vague ones, and the rule to verify anything headed for publication.
  https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01477-w

- **We vibe-coded a custom AI poetry lab. Here's how you can, too.** — *Nature* (2026).
  A concrete, end-to-end researcher case study.
  https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01106-6

- **Scott's Mixtape — "Claude Code for Economists"** — Scott Cunningham (Baylor).
  An economist documenting real agentic research workflows session by session;
  the closest thing to watching a peer do exactly what this course teaches.
  https://causalinf.substack.com/

## Academic framing

- **How Scientists Use Large Language Models to Program** — arXiv:2502.17348 (2025).

## Practitioner best practices

- **Vibe Coding Best Practices: Avoid the Doom Loop with Planning and Code Reviews**

## Cognitive science behind the slides

- Kahneman, D. (2011). *Thinking, Fast and Slow.* Farrar, Straus & Giroux.
- Parasuraman, R. & Manzey, D. (2010). Complacency and bias in human use of
  automation: an attentional integration. *Human Factors*, 52(3), 381–410.
- Rozenblit, L. & Keil, F. (2002). The misunderstood limits of folk science: an
  illusion of explanatory depth. *Cognitive Science*, 26(5), 521–562.
- Slamecka, N. J. & Graf, P. (1978). The generation effect: delineation of a
  phenomenon. *J. Experimental Psychology: Human Learning & Memory*, 4(6), 592–604.
- Lee, H.-P. et al. (2025). The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking.
  *CHI 2025* (Microsoft Research / CMU).
- Kosmyna, N. et al. (2025). Your Brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of Cognitive
  Debt when Using an AI Assistant for Essay Writing Task. *arXiv preprint*
  (MIT Media Lab). Note: a preprint with active methodological debate — treat as
  suggestive, not settled.
